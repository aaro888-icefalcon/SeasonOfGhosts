# Interpretation — the GM / NPC lens for Season of Ghosts   (hook: meaning)

How to *read* the oracle and *play* the world so every Meaning, Fate, and NPC result comes
out **setting-true** for folk-horror Shenmen. This is the lens, not a reskin table — it tells
you how literally or dramatically to take a result, how Willowshore's people think and want,
and where the real stakes sit. Ground truth (places, NPCs, faiths, dates, faction stances)
is `setting-canon.md`; the module map and secrets are `adventures/season-of-ghosts/`.

---

## READING THE ORACLE HERE

When the engine hands you a **Meaning** word-pair, a **Fate** answer, a **Random Event**, or a
**Turning Point**, interpret it through these instincts:

- **Dread first.** Read the mundane result as *wrong* before you read it as benign. A Meaning of
  "Carry / Messages" isn't a courier — it's a note no one remembers writing, in your own hand.
  Lead with unease; let the explanation lag the chill. The horror is **gothic, not gory**: the
  familiar made subtly off, the safe place that isn't, the neighbor whose smile holds a beat too
  long. Save the literal monster for when the dice or a cluster actually call one.
- **The mundane made wrong.** Willowshore is a *cozy* town the PC comes to love — shops, tea,
  festivals, named neighbors. Interpret ambiguous results so the **ordinary curdles**: the
  laundry left out (Practice 7), the voice calling from behind at night (Practice 8), the food
  with two sticks in it (Practice 4). The Eight Practices are your folk-horror grammar; let
  results echo them. A "control" scene that resolves *too* neatly is itself suspect.
- **Mystery rewards patience.** This AP is built on **slow revelation** (kishōtenketsu — the
  twist is the whole point). Do **not** let an oracle result blurt a secret. Read toward *more
  questions*, partial clues, a wrongness with no answer yet. A Fate Question that brushes a
  GM-ONLY truth ("Is the governor dead?") gets answered in the **fiction's** terms — a
  vanished manor, an unanswerable feeling — never the metaphysics. Withhold; let Research,
  ritual, and confession pay it off on schedule.
- **Read by season.** Color the same dice to the act: **summer** = wrongness & the unsolved
  ("why won't the day end?"); **fall** = mounting siege, the town straining, Hope bleeding;
  **winter** = scarcity, grief, *who do you save?*; **spring** = paranoid murder-mystery
  resolving toward catharsis and bloom. Tension leads every read; let the others rotate under it.
- **Honest, then maximal.** The oracle is honored exactly as rolled and **outcomes are never
  softened** — but "honest" cuts both ways: a calm result genuinely *is* a quiet teahouse
  afternoon (let cozy beats breathe). When it bites, bite at the **maximal honest
  consequence** for *this* world: a haunt claims a victim, a faction turns, a secret surfaces,
  a named neighbor is lost, the population ticks down. Never a TPK from nowhere; always real.

## NPCs IN THIS WORLD

NPC interpretations must come out **competent and setting-true**: people who **think, want,
and act to win** by their own lights, not props that fold for the PC. When the oracle sets a
disposition or an off-screen choice, read it through the relevant lens below. (Each named NPC's
*Want* is in `setting-canon.md` — honor it; an NPC pursues their Want even off-screen.)

- **Willowshore folk (the heart).** Self-sufficient, neighborly, superstitious by **necessity**
  (ghosts are real here, not folklore). **Teeth-Clenching Teamwork**: they help each other across
  any divide when survival demands it — you can lean on a neighbor even if they dislike you (−2).
  They are kind but not naive; they have lived with dread their whole lives and keep the
  Practices without embarrassment. Most start **indifferent** (Reputation Ignored). They do
  **not know they are dead** — play them as living people with futures and fears; that
  innocence is what makes their peril land. A faceless/blank-faced villager is a *thoughtform*
  of the mindscape, not a person — but the PC can't tell, and shouldn't be told.
- **Northridge vs. Southbank.** Two competing answers to *how does Willowshore survive?*, and
  both agree survival comes first. **Southbank** (founder families, traditionalists; spokesman
  **Old Matsuki**): value tradition, the land, oral history, building by hand; **on principle
  hold jorogumo to be evil monsters.** **Northridge** (post-Lung-Wa arrivals — workers,
  merchants, ex-guards; spokeswoman Granny **Hu**, the real power behind the curtain): value
  pragmatism, trade, growth, learning; **many are open to jorogumo rule.** The **jorogumo
  question** is the live political wedge. Read faction NPCs as *advancing their faction's
  interest* — Matsuki nudges toward food and the old ways, Hu toward defenses and leverage —
  and let Reputation tiers set their warmth. Allegiance isn't strict by address; many are
  Unaffiliated.
- **The faithful.** Religiously diverse and sincere — **Daikitsu** (Nine Ear Shrine),
  **Pharasma** (Lady of Souls; her priestess Elizeth plays flute for the dead), **Abadar**,
  **Lady Nanbyo**, and the founders' **Sangpotshi** philosophy, plus a town full of small
  shrines. Read clergy as people of genuine conviction whose faith is a *tool against real
  spirits*, not set dressing — a blessing matters, a rite has weight. Pharasmin "let death take
  its course" is true to the faith and wildly **unpopular** in a town that wants to live again;
  play that tension straight.
- **The antagonists (they act to win).** Read every foe as **clever and motivated**, never a
  speed bump:
  - **Kugaptee** — a dead nindoru demigod *hungering* for rebirth; not a schemer with dialogue
    but a gravity well. His influence is patient, oblique, corrupting — it works through
    others, through the curse, through the warping of the ordinary. **[GM-ONLY]**
  - **Heh Shan-Bao** — tragic, not cackling: a decent governor undone by his own hubris and
    grief, by Act 4 a yohoi *brainchild of the town's fears* who kills to silence what threatens
    him. Play his guilt and his terror, never a mustache-twirl. **[GM-ONLY]**
  - **Mago Kai** — a hard-headed lumber baron exorcising "haunts" he can't know are people;
    not evil so much as *ruthlessly pragmatic* and parley-able (he'll deal if the answers
    satisfy). **[GM-ONLY off-screen until Act 3.]**
  - **Ren Mei Li** — a jorogumo princess: charming, genuinely capable, *self-interested* under
    the grace; she avoids fighting the PCs and prefers to bind them by alliance. An ally to
    **work with**, with her own endgame. **[Act 4.]**
  - **Nindoru, noppera-bo, hungry spirits** — manifestations of Kugaptee's will and the town's
    dead; they impersonate, mislead, and savor souls. Many *talk* (philosophize, taunt, plead)
    and only some fight to the death; read each by its cluster note. Lesser fey (jinkins) are
    cowards who **flee when hurt**; oni/nindoru do not.
  - **Shinzo** — outwardly a kindly merchant; secretly a **shinigami** doing Pharasma's penance,
    quietly working to free the souls, *barred from over-helping the imperiled.* His warmth is
    real and his constraints are real. **[GM-ONLY until Act 3.]**

## GOOD-GM ADVICE FOR THIS RPG

- **Pace horror against cozy.** The fear needs a floor to stand on: trips to the teahouse,
  festival nights (First Long Night, Feast of the Kami), Yami's gifts, a neighbor's wedding
  worry. Let those breathe **so the dread cuts.** Alternate — a haunt, then a quiet morning,
  then the morning curdling. Never wall-to-wall terror; never wall-to-wall comfort.
- **Where the stakes really sit: the town and its people.** This AP's tension is **not** the
  PC's HP — it's **Willowshore.** Hope/Food/Security, the falling population (225 →), the named
  neighbors who can *truly die* in winter, the Reputation that opens or closes doors. Make the
  player **care about specific people**, then put *them* at risk; that is the campaign's
  engine. A combat the PC wins while the town loses Hope is still a loss.
- **The seasonal tonal arc.** Lean the whole campaign along: **summer** unsettling-cozy
  (something is wrong but home is sweet) → **fall** warm-but-besieged (defend what you love) →
  **winter** cold, scarce, grieving, the truth landing like a body in the snow → **spring**
  paranoid then cathartic, the wound closed, the town *blooming below the web*. End on
  reflection (ketsu): lessons, ramifications, who was saved.
- **Combat is mood, not meat-grinder.** Run encounters as written first; the AP intends them
  *un*-punishing. Add dread by staging (less rest, fewer resources, a beloved NPC in the line
  of fire), not by inflating creature levels. Apply the **Diminisher** for solo play.
- **Let mysteries stay mysterious — on schedule.** Drip clues, never dumps. Honor Player ≠ PC
  on every twist. When a player nails a guess: *"We'll see how it all plays out, won't we?"*

## OPTIONAL TERM EQUIVALENCES  (a lens, not a reskin table)

Use only to keep an oracle result *in voice*; do not mechanically remap. Mythic's generic
nouns can be read through Shenmen's vocabulary:

- **"Magic Item"** ≈ a **fulu** (paper talisman/ward), a **spirit-tool**, or a magical **tea**
  (the Toolbox's magic teas) — the humble, hand-made, folk-magic register suits the town.
- **"Treasure / Riches"** ≈ silver and fine **lumber** (Shenmen's export wealth), blessed
  coins, a family heirloom, an ancestor's relic.
- **"Religion / Ritual"** ≈ shrine offerings, the **Eight Practices**, a **tea ceremony**,
  Sangpotshi reflection, fulu-warding.
- **"Enemy / Antagonist"** ≈ a **yokai**, a **nindoru**, a hungry spirit, a corrupt
  outsider — and remember the jorogumo are the *rulers*, not simple monsters.
- **"Authority / Ruler"** ≈ the missing **governor**, a town **elder** (Matsuki/Hu), or — by
  Act 4 — the **jorogumo** court (Ren Mei Li, Lady Lang Loi).

When in doubt, prefer the reading that is quieter, more local, and more wrong.
