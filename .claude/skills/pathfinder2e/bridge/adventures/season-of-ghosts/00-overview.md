# Season of Ghosts — Campaign Spine (spoiler-aware overview)

The map of the whole Adventure Path: the four-act arc, the central secret, and how to run
it as **pure sandbox** with the mythic-gm engine. Read this first; then the engine surfaces
the right act/cluster on its own via `index.json`. Detail lives in the per-act slices
(`act1-summer.md` … `act4-spring.md`), the `bestiary.md`, `setting-canon.md` (ground truth),
and the `_index/*.json` cluster files. This file is for **you, the GM** — most of it is
GM-ONLY and must never be volunteered to the player.

The structure is **kishōtenketsu**, the four-act framework, NOT a three-act beginning-middle-
end: Act 1 introduces (ki), Act 2 escalates (shō), Act 3 turns on a twist (ten), Act 4
resolves and reflects (ketsu). The campaign opens on the first day of summer, 7108 IC, in
the isolated lumber town of **Willowshore**, Shenmen, Tian Xia. Tell the player up front that
the AP holds plot twists; if they happen to know one, keep it to themselves.

## THE FOUR-ACT ARC (player-safe spine)

What the player and PC experience, act by act. (The deeper truth under it is the SECRET
SPINE below — kept apart on purpose.)

- **Act 1 — Summer: "The Summer That Never Was"** (≈ levels 1–4)
  The PCs wake in a forest clearing east of town (W38), this year's Reenactment-Festival
  "abductees," to find Willowshore overrun by sapient, hostile monsters and bathed in a
  curse of mist, a crimson moon, and blood rain. They fight home, relight the **Eternal
  Lantern** on Dawnstep Bridge (three coins blessed at three shrines), liberate the town
  district by district, ally with one of the two town elders (Old **Matsuki** / Southbank or
  Granny **Hu** / Northridge), and discover the governor and his manor have simply
  **vanished**. Pushing outward to ride for help, they hit an impassable **mindscape border**
  ringing the hinterlands: they are trapped. The hook is *what is wrong here?*

- **Act 2 — Fall: "Let the Leaves Fall"** (≈ levels 4–5)
  A community-survival act. The PCs lead Willowshore in **preparing for winter** — three
  banks of Preparation Points (**Hope / Food / Security**) earned through weekly downtime —
  while the season's hauntings escalate week by week, each supernatural event draining Hope.
  They restore the **Cerulean Teahouse** (their base) and befriend the cat **Yami**,
  **research the curse** (10+ Research Points), then pierce the western **Wall of Ghosts** and
  walk the **Pilgrim's Path** to the ruined **Tan Sugi Monastery**, where they purify four
  desecrated statues, free the founder's ghost **Zhi Hui**, and reseal the buried fiend
  beneath the great sugi tree. The hook is *hold the town together; learn what cursed it.*

- **Act 3 — Winter: "No Breath to Cry"** (≈ levels 6–8)
  The turn. A cruel winter **drains** the points banked in fall (Hope −1d4, Food −1, Security
  −1 weekly; a depleted bank kills townsfolk and the population falls from 225). Aggressive
  ghostly **exorcists** begin tearing the town apart. The PCs research a way to reach the lost
  governor, cast **mindscape shift** into his parallel mindscape, chase him down, and in his
  warded manor hear his **confession** — the campaign's central reveal. **Shinzo** the merchant
  finally tells them what he is and what they are. They complete the **transmigrate** ritual,
  their road home. The hook is *grief and scarcity — and the unbearable truth.*

- **Act 4 — Spring: "To Bloom Below the Web"** (≈ levels 9–11+)
  Resolution. The PCs **wake alive** in a finely-grained, real spring, 115 years later. They
  cleanse the ruined manor (a nindoru impostor wearing the governor's face), reopen trade by
  rebuilding **Karahai Bridge**, break the **Silkwasp** bandits' jiang-shi master, and treat
  with the jorogumo princess **Ren Mei Li** — an ally to *work with*, not an enemy — completing
  her **Five-Season Treasures** to earn the **Fang and Key**. All the while a serial killer
  stalks Willowshore: the governor himself, returned as a **yohoi**. The Fang and Key opens the
  restored manor's hidden door to the final confrontation that stops Kugaptee's rebirth for
  good. The hook is *unmask the killer; close the wound; let the town bloom.*

## THE SECRET SPINE  **[GM-ONLY — do NOT reveal except through earned play]**

This is the engine of the whole AP. The clusters tag these beats `spoiler: true`; the engine
will surface them as **fragments you read, not lines you speak**. Reveal each only at its
gated moment (named below). The player must figure it out; you neither confirm nor deny early
guesses — *"We'll see how it all plays out by the end, won't we?"*

- **The PCs are dead, and Willowshore is a mindscape.** On the last day of spring **7108**,
  Governor **Heh Shan-Bao**, learning the jorogumo had seized Shenmen, attempted a ritual to
  heal the sacred Tan Sugi and seal the fiend beneath it. It **backfired and killed the entire
  town in their sleep.** Their souls were trapped, unaware, in a **cyclic mindscape** that
  resets every last-day-of-spring to that first day of summer. The PCs have unknowingly relived
  7108 **115 times.** The true present is **7223 IC**. (Reveal: the governor's confession,
  Act 3 / *Know and Despair*; reinforced by *Shinzo's Answers*. Drip Act-1/Act-2 clues — the
  border, the vanished manor, the unsleepable monastery — without ever resolving them.)

- **The governor is the villain — and the victim.** Heh's ritual didn't just fail; trying to
  *control* the fiend "like a mere spirit," he was **devoured, body-mind-soul.** *"I am
  Kugaptee, the beast that slaughtered you all."* A second nested mindscape (a haunting within
  a haunting) holds the last flickers of his individuality; each cycle he runs **transmigrate**
  to save his people and forgets by the reset. By Act 4 what remains of him has become a
  murderous **yohoi**, the brainchild of the town's own fears. (Reveal: Act 3 confession; his
  identity as the Act-4 killer is unmasked via the path maiden's mirror and the murder clues.)

- **Kugaptee and the Web.** **Kugaptee** is a **nindoru demigod** — a once-oft-reincarnated
  evil soul torn from the cycle of souls — sealed ~1,000 years ago by the hero **Tan Sui-Jing**,
  whose soul reincarnated into the **great sugi tree** that pins his grave shut. Dead but
  hungering for rebirth, his straining is the **true source of every monster, haunt, sapient
  beast, and mindscape** in the AP. The "curse" the PCs research is really *his* influence
  bleeding upward — while the *hauntings* of fall and winter are partly the work of real living
  **exorcists 115 years in the future**: lumber baron **Mago Kai**, sent by ghost-ruler **Chou
  Mingxia** of Sze, cleansing the ruins, his work seeping into the mindscape and accidentally
  *empowering* Kugaptee. If the Tan Sugi is felled (or rots from his influence), Kugaptee
  escapes into the living world. (Reveal: Research milestones, then Zhi Hui at the monastery,
  then Shinzo. The name **Kugaptee** stays GM-ONLY until research/the grave earns it.)

- **The jorogumo backdrop.** Shenmen is ruled by **jorogumo** (spider-women yokai) under Lady
  **Lang Loi** since 7107 — at the AP's open, only a far-off "scary rumor" no jorogumo has yet
  visited Willowshore. By Act 4's living present it is the established order: silver collars,
  tribute, scarred children. Princess **Ren Mei Li**, whose web includes Willowshore, becomes a
  **key ally** — pragmatic, benevolent-ish, privately angling for her own power; frame her as
  someone to bargain with. (Jorogumo loathe tengu — see safety notes.)

- **The way home.** Salvation is **transmigrate** (built on Heh's notes + reincarnate/resurrect
  formulas), powered by the relit **Eternal Lantern**, returning the trapped souls into living
  bodies. While Mago Kai holds the Lantern's flame at Karahai, only a temporary spirit-return is
  possible; returning the flame and completing the ritual is the true escape into Act 4.

## RUNNING IT AS PURE SANDBOX (the engine, not a railroad)

**The module is a map, not a script.** This bridge is **atomized**: every chapter is broken
into **clusters** (a scene/situation) of **fragments** (individual plot points, each with
themes, a weight, a page cite, and a spoiler flag). You do **not** read the AP in order.

- **Clusters surface by relevance.** When the engine generates a scene, advances a thread, or
  the player drives somewhere, consult `index.json` / the act slices for the cluster whose
  `location`, `threads`, `characters`, or `gate` matches the current fiction. Offer its
  fragments as *material* — the situation, the foes, the DCs, the secrets — and let play
  decide what happens. A cluster's **`gate`** is the fictional precondition (e.g. "Eternal
  Lantern lit; entering downtown"); don't surface gated content before its gate is met.
- **Honest dice, always.** Every roll runs through the engine's scripts and is shown and cited
  — Fate Questions, Chaos, scene rolls, and all PF2e resolution (`system-profile.md`). The
  oracle is honored as rolled; outcomes are never softened (`interpretation.md`). Degrees of
  success are real; **death is real** (no GM rescue; only the player's Hero Points).
- **What PF2e resolves vs. what the oracle answers.** PF2e owns combat, checks, saves, spells,
  exploration/downtime activities, and the **subsystems** the AP leans on hard — Preparation/
  Victory Points, Research, Reputation (Northridge/Southbank + per-NPC), Influence, Chases,
  Infiltration, and **Hauntings** (haunts have AC/saves/Stealth and a disable routine). Defer
  to a **Fate Question** for off-screen NPC choices, "does X exist," and any world fact the
  rules don't cover.
- **Theme & chaos lean** are fixed for the campaign: Tension-led folk-horror
  (`theme-weights.md`), harsh control-lean with seasonal floors (`chaos-tendency.md`).
- **The act framework is a pacing tool, not a gate.** Players can linger in summer or rush;
  the clusters carry their own prerequisites (level, ritual, Research Points, season). Let the
  sandbox breathe — cozy interludes in town are *allowed* between dread.

## THE DIMINISHER (solo / off-size play)

The AP is built for a **party of 4** at moderate budget. This companion defaults to a **single
PC**, so apply a **Diminisher of ½** to encounter scale: roughly halve identical foes, or drop
a lone foe ~2–4 levels — pick one Diminisher and use it consistently. **Diminisher = 1** for a
full party (no scaling); **≈ ¾** for 2–3 PCs. Record the chosen value in `campaign-state.md`.
See `system-profile.md` and `../references/combat.md` ("solo scaling"). Note the AP's own
design: combats are **deliberately not punishing** (gothic, not survival horror) — the dread
is meant to come from mood and from fear *for the named neighbors*, not from lethal fights;
don't over-tune upward.

## SAFETY — non-negotiable, run before and during play

- **Horror & Consent.** This is a horror campaign. Hold a session-zero conversation about
  comfort; honor **lines** (cut entirely) and **veils** (happens off-screen). If a trope lands
  badly, downplay or remove it. Check in as the seasons darken (grief, scarcity, body-horror,
  death of beloved NPCs). (Paizo "Horror and Consent.")
- **Arachnophobia.** Spiders and **jorogumo** imagery recur as allies *and* foes — it is woven
  into the setting. If the player is uncomfortable, **reskin** spider creatures to another
  unsettling animal (toad, snake, vulture, worm) and keep the art hidden; preserve the dread
  without the phobia trigger.
- **Tengu animosity.** Jorogumo fly into murderous rages near **tengu**. At the AP's start no
  jorogumo has reached remote Willowshore, so a tengu PC almost never faces this, and the town
  itself holds **no** anti-tengu bias (the tengu guild leader Kawaka is respected). *If* a
  jorogumo visit arises (rumor in fall/winter; Ren Mei Li in spring — note she is written as
  tengu-tolerant), **check in with the player first** and handle it comfortably. **Never spring
  it as a gotcha.**

## PLAYER ≠ PC — guard every twist

The AP's power lives in its surprises, so the **discipline that the player's knowledge is not
the PC's knowledge guards every reveal.** Treat each `spoiler: true` fragment as GM-only
reference: you may *act* on it (the world behaves as if it's true), but the PC learns it only
at its gated moment through earned play. Never let oracle results, stat blocks, or this
overview leak a name (Kugaptee, Mago Kai, the mindscape, "you are dead") into description
before the fiction has earned it. If the player guesses correctly, neither confirm nor deny —
a correctly anticipated story is still satisfying. The town behaves as people who do **not**
know they are dead; honor that until the confession lands. This separation *is* the AP.
